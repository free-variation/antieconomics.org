title: "Latouche on Africa"
---

Serge Latouche, whom I just mentioned, has dedicated his life to studying and theorizing about degrowth, working extensively in Africa. I once asked him if his theory of degrowth was largely indebted to his personal experience on that continent; he replied immediately that yes, it was.

One of Latouche's books is titled *L'autre Afrique* (The Other Africa). There is a moment in the book that I want to highlight. Latouche writes that in Europe there are people – surely not many, but they exist – who have a guilty conscience in relation to Africa, i.e. are aware of the aftermath of five centuries of the North’s plundering of the human and material wealth of the continent. This guilty conscience is often expressed as the idea that we must help the people of Africa. Latouche provocatively asks: wouldn’t it instead be smarter to let them help us? On numerous occasions, and in conditions of extreme poverty, the inhabitants of Africa have demonstrated their ability to develop networks of solidarity that have effectively solved many problems. In the rich North, on the other hand, we have quite dramatically lost this capacity. Perhaps we are still in time to recover it.

If we want to help Africa \- Latouche continues, provocatively \- the best thing we can do is leave its inhabitants alone. They were much happier five centuries ago, before the arrival of our ancestors. That said, if we have a vaccine against AIDS, we should give it to them. Without, of course, letting business get in the way.
