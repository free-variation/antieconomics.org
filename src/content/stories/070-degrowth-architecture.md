title: "Degrowth architecture"
---

Verónica Sánchez – I've mentioned her before – is an architect specializing in the design of field hospitals in areas afflicted by wars or disasters. She works in Madrid at a studio that operates within the orbit of the degrowth movement. Though it might seem hard to imagine, it’s possible to develop an architecture that challenges the whirlwind construction of paraphernalia, and destruction of the natural environment, that indelibly marks the everyday practice of the discipline.

One day she told me a story, which I believe I'm not distorting too much, about her studio entering a competition in a Swedish municipality. The challenge was to determine what should be done with a space on the outskirts of town. The Madrid studio won the competition with a groundbreaking proposal: to do nothing.  That is, to leave the space reasonably as it was, untouched; or alternatively – I imagine – to remove any unfortunate elements that human intervention might have left on the land. However, it seems the outcome was contested by the group that placed second, who in the end, unsurprisingly, got the decision reversed and succeeded in pushing forward – I suppose again – a wonderful and elitist development.

When I heard this story, it reminded me of many discussions I've heard in the anarchist world.  I believe it’s quite common in that world to view the State as an intrinsically physical apparatus that needs to be destroyed. We are to grab picks and shovels to dismantle the buildings – horrendous, by the way – of the *Nuevos Ministerios* \[New Ministries\] in Madrid. Although I have often replied that unfortunately the matter is more complex, since we carry the State in our heads, there’s still an argument for deleting the unhealthy consequences of the actions of our species, as was suggested to the authorities of the Swedish municipality.  And for doing the same with what's inside some heads.
