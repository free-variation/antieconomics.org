title: "Two-ice cream breaks"
---

One of the many common ruses that besiege is to blame others for circumstances that in reality result from our own behavior. In Spain I often hear about the unfair competition of Chinese capital. According to this viewpoint \- whose significance I don’t deny \- Chinese capital disregards considerations of taxes and labor rights, and struggling Spanish businesses cannot compete.

A few years ago I had the opportunity to chat with two Galician businessmen. The first complained bitterly about Chinese competition, saying he was about to throw in the towel. The second, in contrast, confessed that he’d traveled to China many times and was investing most of his resources there. Now the obvious question was: that disloyal capital we call Chinese, isn’t it often our very own capital that, invested in China, seeks to benefit from competition so often described as unfair?

And ultimately, what lies behind the strategy of investing in China? It’s clearly to benefit from much harsher levels of labor exploitation – hard as it is to imagine – than what we see here. Someone told me years ago that a businessman, again from Galicia, had created a significant manufacturing complex in the interior of China. One day, he was told that some employees asked to extend their mid-morning break. They wanted it to be long enough to eat two ice creams instead of just one. The businessman, who was earning substantial profits, was willing to grant them a break long enough to have three ice creams, or even four. But the matter didn’t progress further: the local party organization and the branch of the official union advocated for the dismissal of such indulgent and gluttonous people. No wonder China is so appealing to entire segments of the European business community\!
