title: "Thinking about collapse"
---

When I speak about the impending collapse, I often employ a pair of vignettes that illustrate aspects of our attitude towards it. The first is about someone at a port who needs to travel by boat to an island. A friend informs them that there's a 7% risk of the boat sinking during the journey. It's obvious that no one in their right mind would board such a boat, unless they had an absolutely urgent need to reach the island immediately. Nevertheless, though I firmly believe that the risk of a general collapse of our system is well above 7% percent, every day we still get on that boat.

The second vignette asks us to imagine that tomorrow, a prestigious research center predicts that without doubt in 2072 a gigantic asteroid will collide with planet Earth. Logically, the day after tomorrow the system of the United Nations, the governments of different states, the armed forces, research centers, and universities would begin working to determine what can be done to avoid this impact. Although the conditions that are beginning to crush us in the form of climate change and the exhaustion of energy raw materials, are increasingly comparable to the mass of the asteroid, we prefer to look the other way. Perhaps this is because the impending collapse is the product of human action through a system called *capitalism*. And not a natural disaster.
