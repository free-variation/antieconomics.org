number: 39
title: "Miseries of resilience"
---

In one of her writings, Verónica Sánchez draws attention to the unique situation of the Batanes Islands in the Philippines. This small archipelago, frequently battered by typhoons, serves as a model of what is known as *resilience* — the ability to withstand pressures and problems and to maintain one’s essence in the face of them. As soon as a typhoon has passed and left its destruction, the rebuilding process begins immediately, without waiting for external aid. The local culture is rooted in a blend of autonomy, austerity, and strength, underpinned by community work and mutual aid. Verónica notes that for the island's inhabitants, failing to rebuild their own home or not aiding neighbors in doing so is considered shameful. They maintain a culture that, in all aspects, from food to construction, withstands the tests of the weather and of time.

In the affluent world, there is often talk of resilience, a concept associated with design and urban and territorial development. Cities frequently implement plans for this purpose. These plans blend wealth, safety, tranquility, and comfort on one hand, with the fragility and vulnerability of the inhabitants on the other. These populations depend on macro-systems that are easily unbalanced by minor problems or simple interruptions in supply. The mentioned plans seem destined to fail if, as is common, individual training is neglected. “It's the price of well-being,” Verónica comments, “of an education focused on happiness, of security as a coveted way of life, of reduced effort, of luxury understood as comfort. It is a consequence of the subordination of the individual to infrastructures they are not part of, which create a population dependent on external defense and protection systems.”

Living in a constant storm has enabled the inhabitants of the Batanes to face the tempest. Their culture is inertial and spontaneously resilient, and at the same time, all individuals are resilient, with a clear awareness of the need for mutual aid. How different the situation is in many large cities, which claim the label of resilience even though their inhabitants, unfortunately, have little to do with it.
