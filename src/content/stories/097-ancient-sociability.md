number: 97
title: "Ancient sociability"
---

I have always thought that in Portugal an ancient style of sociability has been preserved that in many other places has evaporated. This phenomenon seems to still exist, despite showing signs of gradually receding, under the commercial pressures of the European Union and its associated miseries. I suspect this is happening at the same pace as the decline of the rural world, which is largely at the origin of this type of sociability.

The example of this thesis that comes to mind, however, doesn’t refer to the rural world. Instead, it’s something that took place in a modern train, a product of perhaps the latest technology, connecting the two major Portuguese cities: Porto and Lisbon. In other words, I’m not talking about a 19th-century wooden train.

I was reading in my seat, and after leaving Coimbra behind, I overheard a conversation in the corridor between the conductor and an elderly lady who was speaking in French. The lady, intending to travel north to Porto, had mistakenly boarded a train heading to Lisbon. Half horrified, she asked the conductor what the next stop was. He replied that Lisbon was next, meaning there were no intermediate stations.

In Spain, I’m sure, the conversation would’ve ended there. If someone, very boldly, had dared to ask about the possibility of the train making an extraordinary stop at an intermediate station, they would probably have received from the conductor, in an unfriendly tone, a response like: "Don't ask foolish questions. The computer system doesn't allow it, madam\!" In Portugal, however, the issue was quickly resolved. The conductor called the train driver, explained the situation, and asked to stop at the next station. The driver did so.  We arrived in Lisbon with an unforgivable delay of three minutes. Damn that ancient sociability.
