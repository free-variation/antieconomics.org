title: "The metaphor of the water lily"
---

The metaphor of the water lily is well known. It serves to illustrate how easy it is to be wrong when assessing the damage we have caused to the earth’s environment. And to draw attention to the speed with which processes that escape our control make their effects felt.

Imagine a lake in which there is a water lily. We know that lilies double in number every day: if there is one water lily on Monday, there will be two on Tuesday. Let’s say we also know that the lake will be completely covered with water lilies after twenty days, at which point the plants, lacking vital space, will die off. If I asked you on which day the lake is half-covered in lilies,  you might quickly and spontaneously reason that the answer is the tenth. However, as you’ll soon realize, that’s incorrect. The correct answer is the nineteenth day: since lilies double daily, if they cover half the lake on the nineteenth day, they’ll completely fill it on the twentieth.

On the eighteenth day, the water lilies will cover a quarter of the lake, on the seventeenth an eighth, and on the sixteenth a sixteenth. Let's say we are on the sixteenth. Someone will say: it's not so bad what we've done, since we've only damaged a sixteenth of the planet's surface. However, someone wiser will counter: we’ve set in motion an incredibly rapid process, so that we only have four days left. And the more we postpone taking action, the more the effort, resources, and energy will be required.
