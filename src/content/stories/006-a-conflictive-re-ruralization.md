number: 6
title: "A conflictive re-ruralization"
---

When I speak of degrowth or of future collapse in regions that are traditionally described as depressed, I usually draw attention to a paradox: these very regions are likely to fare better in such a collapse. This is largely because, in their current state, they are the least reliant on energies and technologies that are complex and challenging to access.

Collapse will hit harder in cities than in the countryside.  True, not all cities will be affected to the same extent: those with large agricultural areas around them, access to abundant water, located at crossroads, and possessing an ancient and traditional character, will presumably fare better. An example of the opposite model is Detroit, the once-thriving mecca of the American automotive industry, which has already endured a harsh collapse.

Regarding the countryside, it must be acknowledged that, despite what has been said, the scenario will be far from idyllic. The countryside will undoubtedly feel the consequences of our recent actions: the misuse of land, reliance on monoculture, heavy mechanization, and the general commodification of all relationships 

The challenges facing rural areas in the context of collapse extend further. A few years ago, I had the chance to discuss degrowth in Binéfar, a village in Aragon near the Catalonian border. While not a particularly depressed region, Binéfar exemplifies the broader thesis I've outlined, with a significant caveat. I likely lauded Binéfar for its active agricultural scene, plentiful water, and low population density. So, what was the issue for this town and its surrounding region?  The problem lay \- lies \- in the fact that it lies two hundred kilometers from Barcelona, so that in the event of a general collapse, there's a high likelihood of a mass exodus from larger cities, and even smaller ones, as people seek more manageable living conditions. Should this scenario unfold, the initial benefits enjoyed by a depressed region could vanish, as if by magic.
