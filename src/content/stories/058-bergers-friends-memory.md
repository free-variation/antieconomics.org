number: 58
title: "Berger’s friend’s memory"
---

John Berger, in his book *Pig Earth*, recounts a time he was walking through the mountains with a friend, who was about seventy years old at that time. "As we passed by the foot of a high cliff, he told me that a young woman had died there, falling from the pastures while they were mowing the hay. 'Was it before the war?' I asked. 'In 1833,' he replied."

How short our memory is today; a sign \- I suppose \- of how the things we store in our heads lack intensity. Perhaps the elderly do well in losing their recent memory and retaining the distant one. As for me, for many years I lived under the impression that the most recent event was the Spanish Civil War. That perception, an obvious distortion of reality for someone born in 1956, was largely because the results of the war, Franco’s dictatorship, had no interest for me. Today I find myself at a delicate crossroads because the period closest to me, the so-called democratic Spain, also holds little interest for me. It seems sordid, bland, boring, and hypocritical. At the same time, of course, the Civil War feels very, very far away.

Underlying these lapses in memory, I suspect, is the allure I continue to feel for the interwar period and the wars themselves.  A fascination I definitely would not feel had I lived through those times.  As it is, although some recollections of Galician expressions I heard at home as a young child date back to the early 19th century War of Independence, my memory is much less firm, much less intense, than that of Berger's friend. And I regret that.
