number: 94
title: "The bicycles wins"
---

Our acceptance of increasingly complex societies has a very delicate correlate: we are becoming more and more dependent.  In response to this, I believe there’s no other remedy than to fight for a progressive simplification of the world we live in. Even so, I willingly admit that the concept of simplification has its problems, given that there are forms of complexity that deserve our full respect.

I'll try to explain what I mean, using a specific example: the contrast between the automobile and the bicycle. Manufacturing the former cannot be done by just anyone; it demands high technology accessible only to large corporations. When a car breaks down, we typically cannot repair it ourselves; we need to rely on specialized professionals. To get the car moving requires energy sources that often come from distant countries and are the root of many conflicts. And finally, there's the environmental aggression caused by cars and their associated industries.

In contrast, the circumstances surrounding the bicycle are, fortunately, different. Their production demands far less specialized and much more widespread technologies. It's a bit of an exaggeration, but it can be said that almost anyone is capable of making a bicycle. Repairs are often within our own capacity. And the only energy they require is that provided by our legs, which don’t pollute. There are ample reasons to conclude that, given these conditions, the bicycle is a much more suitable mode of transport than the car to face the challenges of imminent collapse.
