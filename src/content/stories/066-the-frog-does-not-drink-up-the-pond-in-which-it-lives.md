title: "The frog does not drink up the pond in which it lives"
---

"The frog does not drink up the pond in which it lives," goes a proverb. There is no better phrase to portray a balanced survival strategy that thinks clearly and lucidly about the future.

Eduardo Galeano has reminded us that in certain areas of the Amazon – we travel now to the southern part of the American continent – the intelligence of indigenous agricultures is clearly on show. According to Galeano, the soils there are fertilized without decomposition, nourished by countless small pieces of ceramics scattered by the indigenous people, returning to the earth what comes from the earth. In this way, the land is constantly regenerated, "from time to time, from hand to hand."

Finally, I will let another indigenous person speak – I return now to the north of the continent \- who, in conversation with the powerful white man, portrays with clarity their preferences and values:  “Our land is more valuable than your money. It will last forever. It will not even perish in the flames of fire. As long as the sun shines and the waters flow this land will be here to give life to men and animals. We cannot sell the lives of humans and animals, therefore we cannot sell this land... You can count your money and burn it within the nod of a buffalo’s head, but only the Great Spirit can count the grains of sand and the blades of grass of these plains. As a gift to you, we will give you everything we have that you can take with you; but the land, never."
