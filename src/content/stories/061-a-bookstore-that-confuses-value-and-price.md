title: "A bookstore that confuses value and price"
---

Not long ago, I received a message from a woman who worked in a bookstore located in a provincial capital in Spain. The bookstore was to celebrate its fiftieth anniversary the following year. They wanted to commemorate it with various events, and asked whether I’d be willing to visit for a discussion with a local journalist about the current international political situation

I replied immediately, saying I would be delighted to attend, as long as we could agree, of course, on a date for the debate. My correspondent took her time to respond. A few weeks later she sent a very brief message, asking about my fee for participating. I quickly replied that if they had money to pay me, I would gladly accept it, but if they didn't, I would still attend the bookstore's half-century celebration.

I never heard back from the woman. While there may be many explanations for this silence, one stands out to me: my correspondent realized that someone who didn't charge for participating in a debate wasn't high-profile enough to take part in the commemorative events of a prestigious bookstore. As we know, Antonio Machado advised us not to confuse value with price. Too bad I didn't ask for three thousand euros.
