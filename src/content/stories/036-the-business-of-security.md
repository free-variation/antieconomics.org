title: "The business of security"
---

It's interesting to delve into life's paradoxes, and in particular  into the paradoxes of capitalist life. A colleague told me that homes with security alarms are broken into more often than those without. Why? The reason is simple: installing an alarm signals there’s something valuable inside worth protecting – and presumably worth stealing. Additionally, I've been told that the police often take a long time to respond to these alarms. In such circumstances, it’s not surprising that the idea has crossed my mind to start a company that sells expensive signs saying, "We’re sorry: no security alarm is operating in this home."

When I posted about this on social media, I received several stories that, to varying degrees, relate to these paradoxes. I recall a friend telling me about someone who installed a reinforced door in an apartment they were buying with their hard-earned money. The day they were evicted for being unable to pay the mortgage, they decided to rip out and take the door with them. My father's experience was less traumatic. Long ago, he also installed a reinforced door in his house. I felt compelled to point out that the door was by far the most valuable item in his otherwise modest home. Let’s see if they steal it …
