title: "Nono and his stories"
---

*Les aventures de Nono* \[The Adventures of Nono\], a book written by the French author Jean Grave and published in the early 20th century, was very influential in the Spanish anarchist world before the civil war of 1939\. Here, I recall a couple of paragraphs from its pages because they highlight that over a century ago, there were some who already were clearly aware of something important: schools shape our minds according to criteria determined by the rules of private profit and usury.

Nono \[a child of nine\] was asked to dictate a math problem.  He chose one that he remembered from his time in school.   It was about a merchant who, after buying many meters of cloth for a given amount, asked himself how much he should sell it for to obtain a certain profit. The teacher, named *Solidaria* \[Solidary\], responded that yes, the problem was well constructed, but its formulation pointed to selfish practices worth discussing, as they were clearly rooted in the intention to exploit other human beings. And to help Nono understand, she suggested a kind of alternative formulation as follows: “Given that a man possesses so many pieces of cloth and can make so many dresses from each one, how many friends can he please by giving a dress to each of them?”

Kissing Nono affectionately, Solidaria added: “Perhaps you’re too young to see the difference, but when you grow up and can compare, you’ll understand.” If only someone had taken note, over the last hundred years, when writing many of the textbooks that are fed to young people. And if only, occasionally, the owner of so many pieces of cloth in these books were a woman.
