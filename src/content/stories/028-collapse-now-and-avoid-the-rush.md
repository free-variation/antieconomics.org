title: "Collapse now and avoid the rush"
---

I am often asked if we’ll be able to avoid the collapse. I usually answer that, in my intuition \- I emphasize that it’s just that, an intuition \- the answer is no. What I believe is within our capability is to mitigate some of the more negative effects of this collapse and, possibly, delay its full manifestation for a little while.

I must confess, however, that I don’t know if postponing the collapse by a little is in our interest.  This reminds me of a book by John Michael Greer, whose title in English is somewhat humorously *Collapse Now and Avoid the Rush*.  The core argument is that if collapse is inevitable, wouldn't it be better to start adopting the characteristics of a post-collapse society now, while the most dramatic consequences of climate change haven’t fully taken hold and significant reserves of energy resources still exist?  Wouldn't it be smarter, in other words, to start building an alternative society based on degrowth, re-ruralization, de-technologization, de-patriarchalization, de-colonization, de-complexification, and the de-marketization of all relationships? Even if the operation did not fully succeed, we would have learned something, no? And – allow me the irony, which I owe to Antonio Turiel – if things go wrong, we could quietly return to the miseries of a society that is on the brink of collapse...
