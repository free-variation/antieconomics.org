title: "Questions they don’t let us ask"
---

One of the most significant abilities of the system we endure is how it prevents us from asking the truly important questions. I'll provide a very illustrative example of what I mean. 

The prevailing discourse insists that we must seek new energy raw materials to maintain and, if possible, enhance what we’ve achieved. So, what's the main question they manage to prevent us from asking? It goes more or less like this: Are we genuinely interested in preserving what we’ve achieved, or would we greatly benefit from doing away with many of its elements?

Let's suppose one day the President of the Russian Federation, Vladimir Putin, announces that his country has discovered a gigantic hydrocarbon reserve in the Arctic Ocean, capable of maintaining the current global energy consumption levels for an additional decade. Would anyone seriously think we'd use those ten years to critically rethink the irrationality plaguing our societies? I’ve no doubt that in a decade, we would have to face problems very similar to those facing us today. Or, in other words, we would need to revisit the questions that they stubbornly don’t let us ask.
