number: 33
title: "Felipe González, *el olvidadizo* [the forgetful one]"
---

Felipe González, the former Spanish prime minister, was once asked how we’re to address the challenges arising from the ongoing exhaustion of energy raw materials. Three major measures should be taken, he answered: deploying clean and renewable energies, diversifying supply sources, and \- I believe I quote literally \- cautiously reopening the debate on nuclear energy, or, which is the same in González's newspeak, building new nuclear power plants.

Rather than commenting on what González said, I’m interested in drawing attention to what, significantly, he didn’t say. In any scenario of energy scarcity, the first and most basic response must be to call for reducing energy consumption.  Why didn’t González take this first step? It’s possible he was having a bad day – it can happen to anyone – but I believe the main explanation lies elsewhere: Felipe González had been receiving substantial payments from a powerful power company for many years. His silence was a guilty silence: he was simply defending the interests of the company that paid him.

To confront the challenging energy scenario looming before us, we must adopt two major measures simultaneously. First, as González said, we must indeed deploy clean and renewable energy sources, but not to sustain our wasteful and extravagant lifestyle.  Because, quite prosaically, these energy sources are insufficient for that lifestyle. The second measure requires, inexorably, a reduction in energy consumption – something we can easily achieve. Even if it’s not in the interests of the company that paid González.
