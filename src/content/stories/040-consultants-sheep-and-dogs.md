title: "Consultants, sheep, and … dogs"
---

The anecdote, which I've heard many times in more or less similar formulations, is well-known. I believe it serves to highlight the superficiality of the knowledge of highly educated people and the arrogance with which they often operate. It also illustrates the awareness of this fact by many humble people who are perceived as foolish.

The story goes that an executive, presumably well-dressed, arrives in a prairie near the French Alps in an expensive car. He meets a shepherd calmly tending to a large flock of sheep. The newcomer, whom we'll assume is from Paris, asks the shepherd whether he could take one of the sheep if, within two minutes, he is able to guess the exact number of sheep in the flock. The shepherd, though hesitant, agrees to the wager. The executive then activates his powerful computer, quickly obtains a satellite image of the flock, and uses an advanced statistical program. Within seconds, he confidently tells the shepherd, "You have 1,634 sheep."

The peasant, stunned, agrees and hands over the animal to the Parisian. Recovering from his surprise, and as the executive is loading his prize into his car, the shepherd makes a counterproposal: "If I can guess your profession, will you return my sheep to me?" The Parisian immediately accepts the challenge. Without delay, the shepherd says: "You are a consultant, educated at the Paris Institute of Advanced Studies." The executive, surprised, confirms this is and naturally asks how the shepherd was so astute. "Very simple," the latter replies. "You came here uninvited, you provided me with information \- the number of sheep in my flock \- that I already knew, and to top it off, as a perfect illustration of your expertise in livestock, you were about to drive off with my dog, which looks nothing like a sheep."

I don't know what stands out more in this story: the natural intelligence of the peasantry, or the congenital stupidity of many of the people who have been educated, indeed, in an institute of advanced studies.
