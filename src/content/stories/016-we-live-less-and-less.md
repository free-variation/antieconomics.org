title: "We live less and less"
---

On the same topic, our unfortunate use of time, I’d like to add an observation that has a suggestively pedagogical dimension, also because of its graphic character. From the mid-20th century to the present, especially in the affluent North, we've effectively gained an average of three hours per day.  That is, today we live 24 hours for every 21 hours that our grandmothers and grandfathers enjoyed.

And yet, this apparently healthy gain is offset by the more than four hours a day we spend watching television \- in the United States, it's estimated that people spend as much as three entire years of their life watching the idiot box \-.  We dedicate an hour and a half a day commuting to work, tired and with bags under our eyes.  And many of us spend an almost unlimited time navigating social networks.

The conclusion is clear: if we subtract all these hours, which I’ll allow myself to label  as lost time, it turns out that we live, on average, quite a lot less than our grandparents. Perhaps that's why our ancestors, despite the challenging environments they faced, were generally happier than we are today. At least, that's how it seems to me.
