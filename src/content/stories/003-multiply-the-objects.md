title: "Multiply the objects"
---

Forgive me for staying with the straw huts.  And I do so to retrieve one of the texts included in that remarkable book, *Der Papalagi* (The Papalagi)[^4].  According to this text, if a white population – the aforementioned Papalagi – were forced to live in the characteristic huts of Samoa, they would only suffer and complain, and promptly head into the forest to collect wood, turtle shells, glass, wire, colorful stones, and many other objects and raw materials.  They would work day and night to ensure that their cabins were full of things.  The latter, large and small, would break easily and be at the mercy of fire and rain, so they would have to be constantly replaced.  The wisdom of the Papalagi concludes that the more objects you need, the better European you become.

I have read that, on average, those of us living in wealthy countries possess around ten thousand objects. In contrast, many people in indigenous communities in Latin America have merely a hundred or so items per person. However, it's a given that they are not a hundred times less happy than us. On the contrary, I suspect that their happiness is often strikingly greater, much greater, than ours.
