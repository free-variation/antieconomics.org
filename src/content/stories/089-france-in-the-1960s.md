title: "France in the 1960s"
---

When I started working on degrowth, I was deeply impressed by the views expressed at the time by a French essayist named Philippe Saint-Marc. Saint-Marc invited us to imagine a France where there were fewer than 200,000 unemployed, where crime rates were five times lower than today's, where hospitalizations related to mental disorders were reduced to a third of the current figure, where suicides decreased by 50 percent, and where drugs weren’t consumed. This idyllic world, apparently unattainable, was, however, according to Saint-Marc, the France of the 1960s... Similarly, it’s known that when a filmmaker wants to recreate a reasonably quiet urban world, he knows well that one of the easy options is to remove cars, a genuine contemporary plague, from streets and squares.

I dare to suggest that often, and paradoxically, the virtues we attribute to the past are simply the product of an austerity that was perhaps inevitable at the time. Regardless, it seems logical to conclude that more and more people are nostalgic for the economic and social life they once knew – I'm not, of course, referring to the *recent* past: it's also logical that, in times of crisis like the one we're experiencing, there are those who simplistically miss the relative, and false, prosperity of just a few years ago.
