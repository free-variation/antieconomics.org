number: 41
title: "They shot at clocks"
---

The appearance of clocks in church towers in medieval Europe undoubtedly caused radical changes in the perception of time, particularly in terms of work time. It multiplied mechanisms of control as brutal as what the internet and related tools have enabled in recent decades. It’s not surprising, then, that in the early 19th century, members of the Luddite movement in England destroyed not only machines of the first industrialization, but also clocks that had been installed in factories.  The Luddites saw, with perfect lucidity, that clocks were aberrant forms of control over their own time and their lives. Nor is it surprising that, as Walter Benjamin reminds us, during the July Revolution of 1830 in Paris, clocks were shot at. 

There are many fables that give central importance to time. One of them, told by Anthony de Mello, narrates how animals, gathered in an assembly, started complaining that humans were stealing from them. The cow complained about her milk being taken, the hen about her eggs, the pig about its meat and bacon, the whale about its blubber... The only dissenting voice was that of the snail, who declared: "I have something that, above all else, humans would like to possess. Something they would take from me if they could: time." A well-known saying reminds us how strange we, the inhabitants of the northern part of the planet, are: we have clocks – and yet, we lack time.
