---
number: 93
title: "Care strike"
---

Some time ago, during a rather mild general strike in Spain, a colleague and I considered the question of which areas of the economy the strike ought to affect. My friend, somewhat stuck in a past where production was, or seemed to be, everything, couldn't understand why we were also demanding a consumption strike. "What sense would it have," I asked him, "to stop work in factories and schools only to rush to shop at the supermarket later? And what about the rights of those working there?"

Often, however, just when we believe we’ve made a giant leap in understanding the world's problems, we soon realize we've fallen short. Because if the strike is to encompass production and consumption—and indeed, distribution, as the French yellow vests have taught us—what sense is there in talking about redistributing work if at the same time we ignore the vast scope of domestic labor, overwhelmingly carried out by women? Shouldn't we advocate, without reservations, for a strike that affects this type of work as well?

Antonella Picchio explains it clearly: "Feminist thought has shown us how the dominant economy renders much of the work done by women invisible. But the productive process is not sustained solely on the remunerated labor that produces goods and services; it also encompasses the unpaid work that facilitates social reproduction and the sustenance of the working class. Without domestic labor \- cooking to feed families, maintaining clothing and homes... \- and the care of people and their relationships, the economic system could not perpetuate itself. Yet, the growth economy does not consider this at all."

It’s hardly surprising that the idolized indicator of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) doesn’t care at all about domestic work. That indicator, and economic growth itself, have a patriarchal and male chauvinist character. In the eyes of Anselm Jappe, both are linked to masculine values \- toughness, determination, reason, calculation, contract \- while non-market activities are associated with feminine values: gentleness, understanding, emotion, and gratuity.
