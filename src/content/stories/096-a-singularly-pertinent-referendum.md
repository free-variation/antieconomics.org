title: "A singularly pertinent referendum"
---

I include here a text by Gustavo Duch, which, in line with what I just pointed out, reminds again that there are matters about which we’re not allowed to express ourselves. Although it plays stylistically on the aftermath of the Catalan referendum of October 2017, Duch in no way intends to diminish the significance of the latter. His aim is to draw attention to the importance of allowing us to express opinions on otherwise censored subjects, and not to belittle the significance of the Catalan referendum, then and today.

"After many demonstrations and no serious response, society decided to organize a referendum. Logistics such as where voting could take place, which census to use, where to acquire and hide ballot boxes, and how to transparently count the votes were easily resolved through self-management and social collaboration. The question to be posed was collectively decided in assembly processes. Everything was planned, except that on the day of the referendum, following orders from central governments, military and police forces arrived with batons and rubber bullets to prevent the illegal consultation."

"But the vote took place, and the results were known. The vast majority of the population from hundreds of countries responded 'yes' to the question on the ballots: 'In order to eradicate poverty, halt climate change, and avoid the sixth extinction, do you believe that it is necessary to end capitalism?'"
