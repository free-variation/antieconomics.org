title: "The *Quieto* Protocol"
---

In my family’s case, and I suppose this is true for many others in at least moderately rich countries, each new generation travels more than the previous one. I believe my paternal grandmother never left Galicia, and my maternal grandparents moved little more than between Galicia itself, Madrid, and Valencia. My parents never crossed the Spanish borders, except for a brief post-war trip my mother made to Lisbon and, later, with my father, to Porto. While I have set foot in – I won't say actually know – about fifty countries, my daughter, a quarter of a century old, threatens to soon surpass that record. Unless, of course, collapse gets in the way.

Years ago, when there was much discussion about the Kyoto Protocol, an appealing idea gained traction in the environmental community: the notion of adopting the *Quieto* \[stay still\] Protocol instead. Our commitment to the planet called for traveling much less, and enjoying the many attractions of our own local area, or those within reasonable proximity. It’s important to remember that while in a not-so-distant year like 1970, our single planet could reasonably meet our needs, things have since changed for the worse. By 1980, we had used up our annual resource allocation by November 3, by 1990 it was October 11, by 2000 September 23, by 2010 August 8, and by 2019 July 29\. To sustain the lifestyle that humanity – or more precisely, its privileged segments – has adopted, we now need 1.7 planets. And it seems we don’t have them.
