number: 73
title: "There are no cinemas in Albania"
---

I have the impression that in the coming years, many processes, in the context of a foreseeable collapse, will experience rapid acceleration. As a result, I suspect that some of the concepts that have seemingly provided us with certainties in understanding the world will cease to be useful – or, at least, will need to undergo deep revision.

I often remember a story I heard – I believe from Francisco Veiga – at the end of the 1990s. A movie was filmed whose main plot line was that Albania had declared war on the United States. In a gathering of intellectuals in France, in which an Albanian was present, a discussion took place on whether the American film could be shown in Albania or if, on the contrary, it hurt deep national feelings. While some thought the former and others maintained the latter, the Albanian remained silent. When the conversation died down, however, his voice was heard. He simply said: "In Albania, there are no cinemas." The enthralling discussion about the supposed effects of a film on national feelings hinged on a material element that was strikingly missing.

In my view, it's likely that in the coming years we'll find ourselves repeatedly asserting that in Albania there are no cinemas. We’ll need to do this, for example, when someone assumes that new technologies will emerge that will magically solve problems that today seem insurmountable. Given the prospect of collapse, we’ll have to ask where the energy will come from – where the cinemas will come from – that will allow these technologies to operate.
