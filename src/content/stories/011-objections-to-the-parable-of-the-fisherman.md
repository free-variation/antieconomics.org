number: 11
title: "Objections to the parable of the fisherman"
---

The story of the Mexican fisherman is clearly a parable; its content should not be interpreted literally.  I think of it as a clear tribute to the condition  of the fisherman, compared to that of the annoying and commercialized American tourist.  Occasionally, however, I have encountered people who are very critical of this view.

A few years ago, during a public event, an elderly man spoke up to defend what he described as a life, his own, of strenuous work to support a very humble family. I tried to explain what parables are, assured him that I understood his viewpoint perfectly, and ventured to ask if, despite everything, he had ever seen the Mexican fisherman in himself.  And of course I added that the very respectable character of my interlocutor’s way of life would disappear if it involved the exploitation of other human beings.

The second controversy I remember was more substantial. It took place on one of the social networks. A Mexican, who as far as I could tell was an honest anti-colonialist, reacted angrily after watching a video of me telling the story. In his view, I was lamentably ignorant of the grim condition of Mexican fishermen, and in any case, he said, the story was meaningless. Once again I found myself explaining parables, and that the thrust of the story is its defense of the fisherman against the colonial and productivist intrusion of the tourist. Still indignant, he asked me why I chose a Mexican fisherman instead of a Spanish one. I had to answer that unfortunately most Spanish fishermen think the same as the American tourist.  Although I suspect he eventually grasped the parable's true meaning and realized his initial misunderstanding, he chose to block me and leave. How many people in Mexico, fully conscious of the impacts of colonization like this man, might have smiled at the parable, finding a sense of identification with the fisherman.
