title: "Western civilization"
---

I really like a text from Eduardo Galeano from his book *Patas Arriba* \[Upside Down\]. It goes like this: "From the point of view of the owl, the bat, the bohemian, and the thief, twilight is breakfast time. Rain is a curse for the tourist and good news for the farmer. From the native's point of view, it is the tourist who is picturesque. From the point of view of the Indians of the Caribbean islands, Christopher Columbus, with his feathered hat and his red velvet cape, was a parrot of dimensions never seen before."

Galeano himself notes that the first time he was kicked out of class was when, as a very young child, the teacher explained that Balboa, the Spanish *conquistador*, had seen both the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans at the same time a peak in Panama. When the teacher added that he was the first man to have seen both seas at once, young Eduardo dared to ask if the indigenous people were blind…

Clearly, we’re the ones who don’t see. We've built our civilization on the foundation of forgetting, in the best case, and exterminating, in the worst, the people we take to be different. It seems that Gandhi was once asked what he thought of Western civilization. Quite rightly, and with a very appropriate use of the conditional, he replied: "I think it would be an excellent idea."
