number: 15
title: "Saving time, for what?"
---

The contemporary world is full of gadgets that promise to save us time. It isn’t clear, though, what we are to do with the time we have saved. Are we in an endless vortex, in which we spend it looking for even more gadgets to save time?

There is a passage from a famous book by Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, *Le Petit Prince* (The Little Prince), that talks about this.  "'Good morning,' said the little prince. 'Good morning,' replied the merchant. He was a seller of sophisticated pills which appeased thirst. One swallows one a week,  
and one no longer feels the need to drink. “Why do you sell that?” Said the little prince.  “It’s a big saving of time,” said the merchant. Experts have made calculations. We save fifty-three minutes a week.  “And what do we do for fifty-three minutes?”  “We make what we want …,” said the little prince, “if I had fifty-three minutes to spend, I would walk slowly towards a fountain.”

Some people use their time more wisely than the little prince. Take, for instance, the hundreds of millions of human beings who happily spend the time they’ve saved watching television. It’s hardly surprising, then, that we're desperately seeking ways to save more and more time. Fountains and water are of no interest to us.
