title: "Pitiful primitive societies"
---

Years ago, after finishing a talk on degrowth in Logroño, a young man approached me and told me something he’d heard from a family member. If I remember correctly, that person was a Salesian nun. The nun had described, in angry tones, what she understood to be the condition of a primitive community that she’d had the opportunity (or misfortune) to know in some remote place in Venezuela.

As the story, though brief, perfectly illustrated how meanings and demands differ between distinct worlds, I took note of the features that the nun attributed to the primitive community. Here they are summarized: "No one can make them work; when they get the food they need, they spend a whole week talking. They live in a large hut. They have everything in common, and everyone takes care of everything; even the children. They go completely naked, so it's urgent to clothe them. In terms of sex, they do what they please and, as if that were not enough, they have some herbs that prevent women from ever getting pregnant."

I could never have imagined a world as extremely tedious as the one described. And never before did I understand as clearly why we persist in destroying such primitive, unhappy, and unproductive people. How much we have to teach them\!
