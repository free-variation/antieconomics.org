number: 69
title: "Talking is living"
---

I have heard this story several times, in somewhat different versions, but in all cases it’s set somewhere in Sub-Saharan Africa. It could hardly be otherwise.

The protagonist is a peasant who, out of necessity and poverty, traveled many kilometers once a week to the market in a larger town. Surprisingly, and for reasons that will soon become clear, he enjoyed spending a good number of hours there. So much so that he readily woke up early and left after nightfall.

One morning, very early, he laid out on a cloth on the ground the few goods he had managed to gather: two bananas, a couple of tomatoes, three apples, and four onions. Soon after, a tourist appeared and asked how much he wanted for everything. Initially, the peasant, stunned, didn't know what to say. But then he named a price which the tourist accepted. The tourist took out the money and prepared to take the bananas, tomatoes, apples, and onions. But the peasant quickly reacted and refused the tourist's money, who couldn't understand what was happening. Then he got a thorough explanation. "If you buy all this from me, there's no reason for me to stay here. I do come to earn some money I need. But for me, as important as that money is the interaction with the people who pass by and with those who also offer their products. I think I could live without the money I get here, but I could never do without those conversations. If you want to take a banana or a tomato, I'll gladly give it to you, but I can't let you take away everything else."
