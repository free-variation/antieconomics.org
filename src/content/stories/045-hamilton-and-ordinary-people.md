title: "Hamilton and ordinary people"
---

I understand that when I speak about degrowth or collapse, the image that often comes to listeners' minds is that of highly conscious activists from critical social movements. However, we shouldn’t dismiss the possibility that perceptions like mine may spontaneously resonate with individuals who have never been involved – though they might in the future – with such social movements.

Years ago, a book by an Australian professor named Clive Hamilton was translated into Spanish. The book is titled *Growth Fetish* and it’s essentially an exercise in challenging the supposed virtues of economic growth.  Hamilton discusses a report from the European Union about a phenomenon apparently common in Scandinavian countries. Although these countries' high levels of social security are hardly comparable to other regions, the study focuses on a familiar scenario: an older individual loses their job and faces significant difficulties in finding new employment, along with the accompanying psychological distress. The study concludes that in countries like Norway, Sweden, and Finland, it's quite common to find that with public assistance or a pension – possibly amounting to a quarter of previous earnings when under the yoke of slave labor– and by reducing excessive consumption, one can live much better with less.

I want to emphasize what should be obvious by now: Hamilton isn't talking about highly conscious activists from critical social movements, but about ordinary people who – I repeat – seem willing to conclude, with well-founded arguments, that they can live better with less.
