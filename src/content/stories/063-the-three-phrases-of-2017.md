number: 63
title: "The three phrases of 2017"
---

At the end of 2017, I decided to compile the most striking statements that I had come across on social media over the previous months. My list included three that, to my mind, reflect the miserable moment we’re in regarding power, social relations, and knowledge.

The first appeared in a cartoon featuring two elderly individuals. One said to the other something along the lines of: "Who would have thought that our most revolutionary commitment would consist simply in going on living, so that our children and grandchildren may also live, thanks to our pensions?" 

The second I owe to my friend Felipe Zapico, who very aptly asserted that "if banks had to return all that they have stolen, we would find out how much it was." Indeed, banks always win, and when we think we have come out victorious, in the end they walk away with the ball. Why is that? What order of things allows it?

The third, finally, was a succinct phrase that illustrates how by paying attention to important matters,  we disregard others of much greater significance. It went more or less like this: "You, who are so outraged by corruption, just wait till you hear about surplus value." Corruption, while undeniably significant, is far less so compared to the daily exploitation of billions of human beings. But – you see – about surplus value, poor Marx, we speak very little. Or not at all.
