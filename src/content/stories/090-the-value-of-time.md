title: "The value of time"
---

This next anecdote takes place in a remote region of the Brazilian Amazon, inhabited by an indigenous community whose members primarily cut wood with extremely primitive tools. Missionaries who reached this region, noticing the hard work the indigenous people had to undertake, gave them stainless steel knives made in the United States. A couple of years later, the missionaries returned to the region, encountered the inhabitants again, and one of the first things they asked about was \- inevitably \- the knives. The response was immediate: it now took ten times less time to cut wood than before. Assuming then that the inhabitants were now producing ten times more wood, the missionary received an emphatic reply: they were obtaining the same amount of wood as before, and now enjoyed ten times more free time to devote to what objectively leads to well-being and happiness …

It seems as evident as it’s unfortunate that in our societies in the Northern hemisphere, we’ve lost the mental chip that many indigenous communities have preserved intact. A chip that – I must emphasize \- is perfectly rational and respectable. How often do we ask ourselves if the income we earn from an overload of work compensates for the rather meager pleasure provided by the goods and services we acquire?. Isn't time among the most precious commodities?
