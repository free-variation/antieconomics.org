title: "Reading Bakunin in the factory"
---

I don't usually read at home. In fact, I only read on public transport – on buses, in the metro, and on trains. However, I read a lot, because in the city where I live, for better or worse, public transport takes up a lot of time. My only real enemy in these places is the noise, which seems to be increasing in our societies. Even so, I think it’s true that if I knew how to drive I would have read a third of the books I have read. Perhaps that's why \- and for other reasons, certainly \- I'm not too fond  of the increasingly faster journeys they insist on offering us.

I am now reminded of a significant and, unfortunately, now forgotten practice of our ancestors, especially those who couldn’t read.  This practice is all the more surprising given that it took place in workplaces, which today seem to be closed off to such activities.  In countries like Cuba and Puerto Rico – and surely the list could be extended – it was common for wage-earners to collectively pay for one of them, man or woman, to read to them from a platform during work. This would happen for a couple of hours each morning and afternoon. The choice of literature was hardly uncontroversial, featuring authors like Bakunin, Kropotkin, Marx... After the reading, the workers would engage in discussions about the ideas presented until the end of the workday.   What a fine job that reader had.  I wish I could have it.
