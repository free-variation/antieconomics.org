title: "Sweeping one’s own house"
---

"I have noticed," said Mr. K, "that many people are put off by our teaching, because we know the answer to everything. Couldn't we, in the interests of propaganda, draw up a list of questions which appear to us quite unresolved?" Bertolt Brecht wrote this insightfully in his *Geschichten vom Herrn Keuner* (Stories of Mr. Keuner).

Someone clever might respond that such a list would be so long it would irreparably damage the propaganda. On the contrary, acknowledging our limitations seems like a good principle, both in formulating and answering questions. A good principle that also advises to wholeheartedly accept it's unwise to boast about anything. We must first start by discussing what a doctrine is and whether it is healthy to have one, or, at least, to be absorbed by it. Especially if the doctrine in question, as often happens, seems imposed or imported.

Some might suggest that in many areas, paradoxically, our best attributes are our flaws and shortcomings. And perhaps that's why it's unhealthy to despise anyone, and it’s advisable to first sweep one's own house. For hygiene.
