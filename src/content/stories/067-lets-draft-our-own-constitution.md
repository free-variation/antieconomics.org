title: "Let’s draft our own Constitution"
---

I can't resist including here this story that – I believe – illustrates how the Western world always tries to impose its rules and principles without respecting the decision-making autonomy and the sound judgment of those it deems inferior. I heard it from a colleague, Marta Llorente, many years ago.

The story is set in the Amazon, perhaps a century ago. A Brazilian official was tasked with explaining a typical liberal constitution, like the one prevailing in the country at the time, to members of an indigenous community in the rain forest. He met with the leader of that community beforehand, who made him a proposal: "Explain to me what a Constitution is, and I, who better understand the mentality of my people, will transmit it to them without a problem."

They did just that, and the official soon observed that the formula was happily effective. The community leader had understood very well what a constitution is, and had explained its fundamental features in a very graphic way, saving the representative of the Brazilian government a cumbersome and presumably inefficient task. Everything was going perfectly until the official heard the last sentence from his translator: "You have now seen what a constitution is. And you have verified that it’s something beneficial. Let's get to work on our own constitution." A good counter, in every sense, to the colonial convulsion induced by the supposedly neutral laws we promote across the planet.
