title: "Ecology and rulers"
---

The ecological awareness of our global leaders is remarkably weak. Usually, if not universally, they are wholly beholden to the interests of powerful economic and financial corporations operating behind the scenes. At best, they adopt the stance of green capitalism, which views ecology merely as an additional source of profit. Making matters worse, conventional politics is shockingly short-sighted, paying little or no attention to medium and long-term issues.

Let me recount an anecdote that, I believe, symbolically illustrates this. Its protagonist is Alfredo Pérez Rubalcaba, a socialist politician and at the time Vice President of the Spanish Government. In January 2011, he held a press conference in Madrid to promote a measure that, if we want to be generous, was a step in the right direction: the decision to reduce the maximum speed limit on highways and expressways from 120 to 110 kilometers per hour.

Four months later, the same individual called another press conference, to make two announcements.  First, the January measure had produced the desired effects: the Spanish energy bill had decreased significantly, and the number of fatalities on the roads had also notably declined. Pérez Rubalcaba’s second announcement was that, as a result, the decision had been made to restore the highway speed limit to 120 kilometers per hour.

One might have expected someone to pounce on Pérez Rubalcaba's neck for blatantly violating the most basic rules of formal logic. If a certain measure is implemented and announced to have yielded desired results, it seems logical to at the very least maintain that measure, or perhaps even extend it further. The \[right-wing\] Popular Party, which soon took over the Spanish government, was indeed flirting with the idea of raising the maximum speed limit to 130 kilometers per hour... A thing to behold.
