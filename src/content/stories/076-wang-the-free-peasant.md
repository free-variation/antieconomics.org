number: 76
title: "Wang, the free peasant"
---

Here is a story about a Chinese peasant, narrated by Santiago Alba Rico. It goes like this: "Wang, a poor farmer who could barely feed his family, one day found a large earthenware jar and brought it home. While cleaning it, his brush fell inside, and the jar suddenly filled with brushes: brushes upon brushes, and for every one that Wang took out, another magically appeared inside. For a few months, the Wang family lived off selling brushes in the market, and their situation, while still not comfortable, improved significantly. But one day, while taking brushes out of the jar, Wang dropped in a coin, and then the jar filled with coins: coins upon coins that reproduced and multiplied as Wang took them out by the handful.

The Wang family thus became the wealthiest in the village, and with so many coins produced by the jar and with the family so occupied, the Wangs assigned the grandfather, already useless for the pleasures of the world, the task of continuously shoveling them out and accumulating them in a corner, mountains and mountains of gold that increased and renewed at a rate that no spending could surpass. For a few more months, the Wang family was happy. But the grandfather was old and weak, and one day, leaning over the jar, he fainted, fell inside, and died there. And then the jar immediately filled with dead grandfathers: corpse after corpse that had to be taken out and buried with no hope of finishing the task, endless lifeless old men who kept appearing from the inexhaustible bottom of the jar. Thus, the Wang family spent all their money and the rest of their lives burying their dead grandfather a million times over.

What must we say about greed, and the sense of limits, especially when these show up in a closed environment like that of a family that doesn’t look outward, where accumulation and happiness are confused. How hard it seems to be to break free from all this, in China as here.
