---
number: 8
title: "Uncle Arsenio’s milk"
---

The story of Uncle Arsenio shows how the commodification of all relationships unhappily destroys well-functioning systems that generated well-being. Uncle Arsenio owned ten cows that gave him twenty liters of high-quality milk every day. He sold the milk to a local dairy company that paid him fairly and was highly regarded for the quality of its products. Uncle Arsenio lived well, and his business generated income for the veterinarian, his suppliers, the store, the bakery, and the bar.

At a certain point, the dairy company asked Uncle Arsenio to increase milk production. This didn't sit well with him, as he had to take out a loan to buy ten new cows and hire an extra hand. Soon after, the local dairy owners received an offer they couldn’t refuse from a multinational corporation. The new firm soon laid off a large part of the workforce and told Uncle Arsenio he had to lower the price of his milk. These decisions were made far away, and at stake were the dividends that had to be paid to the shareholders. 

Uncle Arsenio had managed to hold on, but now things became difficult. To pay off the loan, he had to let go of his helper and was left alone with the cows at a time when the multinational was demanding an increase in production. As there was no money to buy new cows, the company suggested that Uncle Arsenio buy feed to fatten the animals, even if it affected the quality of the milk. However, with the purchase of the feed, expenses skyrocketed, and our hero found himself in a very delicate situation: he couldn't pay off the loan, he couldn't pay his suppliers, and he wasn't even in a position to live decently himself. He applied for a new loan, which of course was denied, and soon after the bank seized the farm and slaughtered the cows (which surely suffered more than Uncle Arsenio himself).

Today, Uncle Arsenio generates no wealth at all. He receives a monthly aid of 420 euros. He can barely afford a room, electricity, and water, and he frequents the soup kitchen. Meanwhile, the multinational – how surprising\! – closed the milk factory, or rather, moved it to another country where production costs were much lower. We all – they say – came out ahead.
