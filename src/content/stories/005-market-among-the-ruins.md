title: "Market among the ruins"
---

Years ago, I had an experience in which, in just a few minutes, I came face to face with the paradoxes, the unreasonableness, and the misery of our societies.  I had participated in a debate in a village in eastern Aragon, in Spain, and on a Sunday morning I was taken to Zaragoza to catch a train.  With time to spare, my companion suggested we take a walk around the buildings left over from a water-themed exposition that had taken place in the Aragonese capital.  Everywhere there was evidence of what seemed like a shipwreck.  The large sums invested had left no trace other than abandoned buildings and widespread waste.  Not only that: in the area of the former exhibition there wasn’t a soul in sight.

On the way to the train station I discovered that nearby, very close to the ruins I’ve described, was a flea market, a traditional market.  The vendors had erected their stalls by hand, using iron rods.  There were no grand investments and no designer buildings.  Instead, it was the usual, humble, yet remarkably effective system.  Because the market was teeming with people – in contrast with the deserted buildings that had enriched a few and inflated the egos of a few political leaders.  Life was clearly elsewhere.  Close, but elsewhere.
