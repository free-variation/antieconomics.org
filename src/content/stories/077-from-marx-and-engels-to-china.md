title: "From Marx and Engels to China"
---

Many years ago, I came across an article \- don't ask me the title, author, or publication \- that gave me a lot to think about. The forgotten author of this text began by noting that Marx’s and Engels’s forecasts regarding the proletariat in Western Europe, made in the second half of the 19th century, proved to be inaccurate. The German, French, and English proletariat didn’t carry out the revolution that Marx and Engels hoped for.  With a touch of irony, we might say that the European proletariat went into a supermarket and decided to stay, seduced by all its products.

However, the reflection didn’t end there. Our author invited us to consider our present, in which the proletariat as a class has been disappearing almost everywhere. The "almost" is justified partly because a very large human group that resists being labeled as “proletarian” has been accumulating in the coastal areas of China, under conditions that strongly resemble those of Western Europe in the second half of the 19th century. Why not imagine – the article asked – that the Chinese proletariat might eventually carry out the revolutionary task that Marx and Engels assigned to their German, French, and English contemporaries?

What interests me about this argument isn't so much its supposed accuracy, but rather what it points to. Expressed a couple of decades ago, such a thesis would have elicited nothing but pitying smiles. Today, however, in the uncertainty stemming from our difficulty in understanding the world we live in and in foreseeing its future, we have no choice but to consider it seriously. It might just be one of the surprises the aforementioned future holds for us.
