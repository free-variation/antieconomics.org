number: 21
title: "Rats and Mutual Aid"
---

Óscar Horta recalls an experiment that was conducted decades ago at Brown University, in the United States. In my view it demonstrates that feelings of solidarity in response to the suffering of others is by no means unknown among non-human animals, indicating the omnipresent logic of mutual support.

In the experiment, caged rats had to press a lever in order to obtain food.  They soon internalized the mechanism.  In a subsequent phase of the experiment, when the animals pressed the lever, an electric shock was generated in a nearby cage, and the painful effects were visible on the other rats.  On seeing this, the animals in the first cage stopped pressing the lever, even at the cost of not getting the food.  

Horta adds that there are other experiments suggesting similar conclusions regarding solidarity with suffering animals. One of them showed that when given the choice between obtaining food and freeing fellow rats trapped in a water tank, the rats most often chose the latter. I suspect that Kropotkin would have been extremely interested to know this. His theory of mutual aid would have been significantly strengthened.
