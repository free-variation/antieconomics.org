number: 84
title: "The price of potatoes"
---

There's a very old cartoon by Mingote, from the Franco years, which depicts a poor person approaching a wealthy individual and somewhat awkwardly saying: "I believe they've raised the price of potatoes for you." The misery was such that the increase in question didn’t actually affect the poor since these people couldn't even afford potatoes.

I also recall something my mother once told me. In the days when the euro was being introduced, she went down to the shop next to her house and overheard a conversation between the shopkeeper and an elderly lady. The latter asked, "And how much do you say a loaf of bread costs now?" "Thirty cents," replied the shopkeeper. Wide-eyed, the old lady said excitedly, "Give me six loaves." Her memory of many years ago, from the era of the peseta marked by countless hardships, advised buying what clearly wasn't needed, to take advantage of a supposed offer.  But one had to put oneself in the place, and in the life, of the old lady.

I would like to conclude with a Jewish joke: A man says to another, 'If I had been Rothschild, I would have had more money than him.' 'How come?' replies his interlocutor. 'Because I would have continued giving private lessons in the afternoon and would have earned some extra money'... How difficult it is to weigh the economic options and perceptions of people. However obscure they may seem to us.
