number: 51
title: "Our childhood teachers"
---

If there was one thing I resented when teaching at the university, it was the pressure of time. Because of an increasing productivist impulse, the teaching staff was forced to cram complex subject matter into rigid and, of course, short timeframes. As a result, examples that illustrated theory disappeared, and the chance to debate with the students was curtailed. This environment led to the compression of knowledge that was seldom subject to debate.

And yet, the teachers we remember most fondly from our childhood and adolescence are those who managed to resist the pressure to adhere strictly to a curriculum. I'm thinking of those who would devote all the time in the world to topics they deemed important, with immense patience and care, regardless of the efforts required. They weren't overly concerned with rigidly adhering to a curriculum set by bureaucrats at the Ministry of Education. The lack of progress in this area might be shown by our limited response to what has been called the *slow education* movement.

Some time ago, I heard a journalist argue for a movement of a similar nature: *slow journalism*. I know this might seem almost like a contradiction in terms, as it’s almost impossible to imagine journalism detached from urgency and time pressures. Yet, the foundations of this proposal seem solid: why not invest the time needed to conduct three or four additional interviews, to verify a controversial fact, to get to know the facts firsthand on the ground, or to review all the material with a critical eye?

The only movement of this kind that seems to have prospered somewhat is *slow food*, which encourages us to dedicate the appropriate time to our social life, and to question the origin and journey of the food we consume. However, it's hard not to glance suspiciously at a pamphlet from *Slow Food Bilbao*, clearly promoted by some of the most expensive restaurants in the Basque capital, offering slow food to those who have usually always eaten slowly: the wealthy.
