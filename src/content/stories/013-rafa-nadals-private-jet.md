title: "Rafa Nadal’s private jet"
---

It seems that in the perception of many, the two Spaniards whose honor and dignity best reflect the virtues of their country are businessman Amancio Ortega and sportsman Rafael Nadal.  It’s not my place to comment on the former’s business skills and the latter’s racket skills; I want to examine another aspect of the issue.

Amancio Ortega is known for frequently making significant donations to the Spanish public health system, for purchasing specialized equipment. “How nice”, someone might say, with a touch of naivety. These donations are however clearly intended to mask the fact that Ortega faces a myriad of difficulties, because of his fiscal irregularities. And probing deeper, we see that these problems are minor compared to the exploitation of so many human beings \- including, of course, minors \- that has characterized the behavior of Zara’s audacious owner. Many choose to focus solely on the spectacle of the donations, overlooking what lies behind. With the brazen support of most of the miscommunication media.

As for Rafael Nadal, I have an anecdote that graphically illustrates some people’s curious preferences. A few weeks ago, a Madrid newspaper, *ABC,* reported that Nadal often travels on his own private plane. No surprises so far. What was interesting was that, according to the newspaper, there are no luxuries of any kind on Nadal's private jet. (I suppose that, in an exercise of self-restraint, the journalist preferred to not say that Nadal’s plane also has zero environmental impact \- why make a spectacle of it?) As someone has suggested on social networks, the tennis player likely drinks his Moët Chandon out of disposable paper cups. Because, after all, who among us hasn't enjoyed the luxury of a private jet conceived for the humble?
