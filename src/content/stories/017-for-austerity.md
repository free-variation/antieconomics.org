number: 17
title: "For austerity"
---

I have always thought that the word “austerity” refers to a healthy trait. When we say of a person that they are austere, we are unequivocally stating a virtue, especially if we contrast that quality with an adjective like “wasteful”. We’re in some sense rediscovering the qualities of people from another era – who, it’s true, may have had no choice but to be austere. To one degree or another, they made a virtue out of necessity.

However, it seems that our rulers, who aren’t always foolish, have stolen the word from us. I first noticed this years ago, when the European confederation of trade unions called for mobilizations against austerity. The situation became even more apparent when I came across the unfortunate and almost incomprehensible term “austericide.” 

The sleight of hand wasn't hard to decipher: government circles were labeling measures that were essentially obscene and indefensible cuts in social spending as 'austerity.' They were cutting exactly what, ironically, should not be cut, justifying these decisions under the guise of austerity – a concept that, in my view, still deserves respect.  Although I suppose that at this point that battle is already lost. Yet another.
