title: "An oxymoron: sustainable growth"
---

Few words today are as pervasive and yet as vague as *sustainability.* I remember a few years ago, hearing a newscaster pronounce it on a television news program, my mother, already very elderly, commented with excellent clarity that this sustainability must mean nothing, as everyone is for it. There’s no self-respecting political party program that doesn’t proudly announce its commitment to sustainability. Even if the program in question fuels the crudest ecological aggressions.

I’ve no doubt that those who originally promoted the concept of sustainability did so with lucidity and good faith.  They were essentially saying that each new generation must hand over to the next—at least—the same natural capital that it inherited from the previous one.  I won't delve here into the controversies surrounding the concept of natural capital because I believe they are not immediately relevant right now.

A good indicator that a concept once considered disruptive has noticeably degraded over time is the fact that the former President \[i.e. Prime Minister\] of the Spanish Government, José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero, repeatedly fused the noun "growth" with the adjective "sustainable." In his view, it seems, the former gained substance as growth that is sustained or maintained over time. Regardless of Rodríguez Zapatero's views, at the stage we are in, especially in the wealthy North, the term "sustainable growth" inevitably seems like a contradiction in terms.
