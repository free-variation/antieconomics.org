title: "Summers and pollution in poor countries"
---

When someone asks me about the philosophy underlying capitalist globalization, I usually mention a frequent remark by Vandana Shiva, the Indian anti-globalization activist, about a memo that Lawrence Summers, chief economist of the World Bank, is said to have written on the subject of environmental economics.

Larry Summers uses three arguments to justify relocating polluting industries to poorer countries. Firstly, since wages are lower in these countries, the costs of pollution, primarily manifested in an increase in diseases and deaths, will also be lower. Secondly, many parts of the Third World have lower levels of environmental degradation, so his logic is to pollute where there has been less pollution before. Summer writes that “I've always thought that under-populated countries in Africa  
are vastly UNDER-polluted, their air quality is probably vastly inefficiently low  
compared to Los Angeles or Mexico City.” The third argument is that since poor people are, well, poor, they are less likely to be concerned about environmental issues. Summers elaborates: “The concern over an agent that causes a one in a million change in the odds of prostate cancer is obviously going to be much higher in a country where people survive to get prostate cancer than in a country where under 5 mortality is 200 per thousand.” 

Such a grim argument led Vandana Shiva to conclude that, apparently, in the eyes of some economic schools, human life has a different value in the wealthy North and the impoverished South. In a dramatic illustration of the way things are going on the planet, Lawrence Summers held a prominent position in the economic team of U.S. President Barack Obama. A word to the wise...
