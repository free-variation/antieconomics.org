number: 23
title: "Who will pay us our wages on Saturday?"
---

When we consider the difficulties we may face in the future, we forget that those difficulties are set to present themselves – or have already presented themselves – because we’ve succeeded in escaping the dead end we were in, and that, ultimately, we’re illuminating a new world.   This brings to mind the richly ironic lyrics of a French anarchist song, presumably from the 19th century.  After calling for the abolition of capital, the song asked who was going to pay us our wages on Saturday once capital is abolished? It’s quite striking, this refusal to accept that the question of wages hardly matters if capital were to end – and in any case has a different meaning from the inherited one.

I’m not sure if what follows from the above is a frank defense of realism in action or, on the contrary, an equally frank rejection of another realism, this one unmistakably demobilizing.  When I hear someone say something is not realistic, all my alarms go off. And I’m reminded of a statement, loud and rude, by Georges Bernanos, who once said that realism is the good conscience of the bastards. They invoke reality as if it were given by nature and literally unchangeable, when in fact, they themselves have shaped the reality to which they refer, which blatantly serves their lowest and most selfish interests. In these circumstances, I suspect we have no choice but to be proudly unrealistic. And to abolish capital.
